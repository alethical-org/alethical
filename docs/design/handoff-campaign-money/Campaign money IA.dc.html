ON THE HOMEPAGE · TBD

The homepage gets an entry point too, called Follow the money. Where it sits is open until the nav is built and we can see it in place.

One thing is decided: no banner, no announcement strip, no “introducing”. A new capability that needs a launch badge to be found is badly placed.

The drawer is already grouped by verb, so Campaign money slots into SEARCH with no restructuring. It sits second: the two subject indexes first, then the person pair. Find My Legislator stays last because it is the only tool in the group — you give it an address and it returns one person — so it is where someone who can’t name what they want ends up.
Tracking is one destination, not a second copy of the list above. Entity types are filters inside the Tracked page, so nothing in the nav changes as tracking reaches donors, committees or lobbyists.
Which leaves the old TRACK group holding a single item called Tracked — a header repeating its own child. So the group becomes YOURS, holding Tracked and Account: two items earn the header, and alerts or saved searches land here later without another nav decision. Signed out, this group is replaced by a single Sign in. The greyed roadmap rows still under Track — Candidates, News — move into Search’s greyed group.

This is the honest version of a “what’s new” module: whose committee filed, which report, and the period it covers — no amounts. Five rows with five dollar figures is a ranking whether we sort it or not, and these rows show why it would be false: two periods end Jul 20, 2026 and two end Dec 31, 2025, nearly seven months earlier.
Both ends are read off the filing — never an assumed January. An ordinary filer’s period opens Jan 1 by statute; a special-election filer’s does not, and the Jul 11 row is one. Where the start doesn’t resolve the row reads “covers through Jul 20, 2026”; where neither end does, it carries the report and the filing date and no period at all. And no row prints a due date, by choice: a due date on a filed report is noise, and due dates belong where lateness is the subject — the profile’s late state, which prints one.

Names are searched as they were filed, and spellings vary between filings — try a shorter part of the name.
Two sentences and one action. It names no agency, no office and no reason: naming an absence advertises it, and a reader told what is missing starts expecting it. The spelling line is the only thing they can act on, and it is true of every search here.

Minnesota registers a committee per office, so the two committee rows here belong to one person — and a name is never proof of that. The register holds “Fateh, Omar Senate Committee” and “Fatehi, Leili House Committee” one character apart, and they are two different people. So a committee row states the office the committee is registered for and nothing about whose it is; a person appears on a committee only where we have confirmed the link.

03

The committee is the record — and it needs a URL

No detail route exists today. If a finance record can’t be linked to, it can’t be cited, sent, or posted — which caps how far this product travels. Every committee and party unit becomes an addressable page with a Share control, at /money/committees/{name-slug}-{registration number}: the number is the identity and the only thing that resolves, because names collide and registration numbers don’t. There is no standalone person page this release — a sitting member’s money is a tab on the profile they already have, and every other name resolves to its committees. And no surface ever adds one person’s committees together: 9 candidates moved $121,241.64 between committees they both control, filed as an ordinary contribution in both, so a cross-committee sum counts the same dollars twice (#1663).

04

What the money page may never do

Sitting members file on two different calendars, so a side-by-side list sets one member’s seven months against another member’s blank — a comparison of filing schedules dressed as a comparison of money. Our own reports are the one exception: signed, dated and showing their working, they may add up across members.

THE OBVIOUS HOMEPAGE MODULE
CUT
No top raisers, ever

A member on the 2026 ballot filed through Jul 20. A member who isn’t files nothing covering 2026 until Feb 2027. Rank them together and the list measures who is on the ballot, not who raised more. So: no sorting by amount, no total summed across members, and no amount at all on a row that lists more than one member.

OUTSIDE SPENDING
NEEDS LINK

ON A BILL PAGE
CUT
Nothing. On purpose.

The state gives us a donor’s name and employer, never their industry, so an industry total beside a bill is a grouping we invented. Naming the authors’ real donors instead does not fix it: any money beside a bill says the two are connected, and we have no evidence they are.

ON A DONOR NAME
HELD
Payments, not a profile

We refuse to guess that two similar spellings are one person. So a donor page would be a page about a spelling — and it would still look like a page about a human being.

INSTEAD
Payments filed under this name
A list of filings, labelled as filings, with a plain line saying spellings vary and this may not be everything. No “who else they fund” until a person has confirmed the links.
Names of committees and party units link, because they carry a registration number we can identify them by. A name that only got paid has none, so it is searchable and its payments listed as filed — never a page. A private donor’s name is searchable but is not a link to a profile. Our own reports are the one surface that may draw a conclusion from these records, and the link runs one way: a report names a member, and a member’s page never names the report.
06

Five rules that ride along with all of it

Every number carries its as-of date

These are periodic filings, not a live feed. Without the date, a total from eight months ago looks exactly like a total from this morning — and the reader draws a conclusion about a race using stale money.

AS OF AUG 8 2026 · CAMPAIGN FINANCE BOARD
Missing is never zero

“We never imported this” and “they spent nothing” are different facts. Render them identically and an import gap becomes a claim about a politician — which on a trust-first product is the worst failure available to us.

NEVER
$0
INSTEAD
Not reported — no filing imported for this period
A total we can’t show the parts of is two numbers

Minnesota names a donor only once their giving passes $200 for the year. So the payments we can list never add up to the reported total, and the difference is real money — not an error, and not a hole in our data. One figure either understates the truth or claims a total whose parts we cannot show. The Board publishes a reported total per filer and resolves which amended version counts, so both numbers are the ordinary case. We show no total only where that service genuinely does not reach — a candidate who ran in a special election files a second report series it does not return — and then it is the named payments, labelled as named payments, with nothing that looks like a grand sum.

Say what’s missing before they search

A search box is a promise. Someone who types a name, gets nothing, and is told nothing will conclude that person gave nothing — not that we don’t hold the record. The gaps are large and permanent, so they belong on the page in plain words, above the results, not in a footnote.

WHAT THIS RECORD DOES NOT COVER
Nothing before 2015.
Unions don’t report to this board at all.
Donors who gave $200 or less in total for the year are never named.
A published number can change

Filings get amended, and a total can go down next week — in public, during an election. A records product must never quietly swap one number for another. When a filing is amended we say so on the figure itself, and the earlier figure stays readable.

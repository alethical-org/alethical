# 3 Realtor PACs. 12 shared campaigns.

Separate contribution limits and dues-funded election spending raise a question about members’ control over political money.

Florida’s campaign-finance records show 3 Realtor political committees reporting $1,000 payments to each of 12 matching campaign names, all dated February 16, 2024. That is $3,000 per campaign and $36,000 across the shared group.

The $36,000 is a subtotal. Together, the committees reported $55,000 in candidate payments dated that day, including campaigns outside this exact-name match.

The committees disclosed affiliation with the Florida Association of Realtors. State letters dated October 18, 2021 name Deborah Rector as chair, copy David Garrison as treasurer, and list the same Orlando office for all 3.

Their money also moves between committees. The Advocacy Committee reported giving $15,000 each to the Action and Activity committees on February 29, 2024. Both recipients reported those amounts. These later transfers show a funding relationship; they do not establish which money funded the February 16 candidate payments.

## What the $1,000 limit means

Florida’s 2024 law sets a $1,000 limit for a person or political committee giving to a candidate for city office in an election. Primary and general elections count separately. The limit is not a single $1,000 ceiling across every organization with shared leadership.

In a 1988 opinion, Florida’s Division of Elections said contributions from separate political committees with substantially the same contributors are treated individually. That opinion helps explain the separate limits; it is not a ruling on these 2024 transactions. Florida also prohibits giving through or in another person’s name.

A $1,000 committee limit can coexist with $3,000 from an affiliated group. The records support a question about how the limit works across a network.

## Dues fund a separate route

The National Association of Realtors describes 2 different channels. RPAC uses voluntary donations for direct candidate contributions. After the 2010 Citizens United decision, NAR created its Congressional Fund to use member dues for independent election spending: spending that supports or opposes candidates without coordinating with their campaigns.

Citizens United allowed corporate funding of independent election spending. It did not remove the ban on corporate contributions directly to federal candidates. Nor does it, by itself, explain the Florida committees’ direct gifts.

NAR proposed a $40 annual dues increase in 2011, approved it that May, and began collecting it in 2012. The increase supported a broader political advocacy program that included election spending. Saying the entire $40 paid for election ads would overstate the evidence.

Declining an optional RPAC donation does not stop NAR from using membership dues for its separate election spending. These national funding records do not trace a Minnesota member’s dues to the Florida payments described here.

## When membership is a work requirement

eXp Realty’s public Minnesota agent guidance requires membership in a local Realtor association, which includes state and national membership. Its August 2026 U.S. policy allows an exception through a written addendum to the agent’s contract. The same policy says unpaid association bills can lead to removal from eXp.

That is a brokerage policy example, not a claim about every agent or a state licensing requirement. The Minnesota guidance has no reliable update date, and its linked state policy manual was unavailable. The dated U.S. policy does not establish every Minnesota-specific exception or how often waivers are granted.

Members should be able to see what their required payments support. That includes who makes political spending decisions and whether members can decline that spending while staying with their brokerage. This transparency standard should apply to Realtor associations, unions, and other organizations spending members’ money in politics. Their legal duties and members’ rights may differ.

## How this was calculated

The Florida calculation uses the 3 committees’ official first-quarter 2024 expenditure exports, copied October 8, 2026. We selected candidate payments dated February 16 and matched campaign names exactly across all 3. Each of the 12 matches has 1 payment of $1,000 from each committee: 12 × 3 × $1,000 = $36,000.

The 12 matching names are Alex Cull, Berne Barton, Bruce Rector, Christina Lambert, Gary Price, Mike Mannino, Mimi May, Nicholas Coppola, Ryan Boylston, Shelly Albright, Tony Perez-Benitoa, and William Kramer, as recorded in the exports. We included Bruce Rector’s Action Committee payment, coded MON rather than CAN, because the recipient and purpose identify a candidate payment. We did not merge Rick Butler with Ricky Butler: the ledgers use different names and addresses, and a city release identifies Rick as the retiring council member and Ricky as an incoming council member. Recipient-side evidence would be needed to resolve that mismatch. The full candidate-payment totals for the date are Advocacy $28,000, Action $14,000, and Activity $13,000, adding to $55,000. February 16 is the payment date shown in the exports, not an established filing or bank-clearing date. These are current database exports; their amendment history was not established. Organizational letters establish the shared details in 2021, not uninterrupted leadership through today.

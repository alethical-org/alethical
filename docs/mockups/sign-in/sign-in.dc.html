THE THIRD FORM
Same black pill, no words. Any label would assert a state, so the unknown form drops the label and centres a small white spinner in the same black box at 62% opacity. Black keeps it recognisable as Track; the motion says "deciding", not "broken"; the dimming says "not yet yours to press". It is disabled with aria-busy="true" and an aria-label of "Checking your tracked bills", so a screen reader is told the same thing sighted users are.
The footprint is fixed for all three forms, not just this one. Each size gets a min-width set above its widest form ("✓ Tracked") with content centred, so subpixel rounding and font fallback can't reintroduce a shift: 128 × 46 desktop (widest shipped form 127.31), 112 × 44 phone (107.48), 124 × 44 compact (116.28). Desktop needs an explicit height, not a min-height — a 16px/600 label makes a taller line box than an 18px spinner, so a min-height binds on the spinner form only and the box would shrink 1.5px on resolve. These numbers are the whole guarantee; measure all three forms after building and confirm they're identical.
This also fixes an artifact nobody flagged, and it is bigger than it looked. "+ Track" and "✓ Tracked" are different widths in every size — measured on shipped code, compact runs 100.13 → 116.28px, a 16px jump, and desktop and phone jump 16–18px too. So every ordinary track and untrack currently shoves its whole row sideways by that much. Locking the box removes it everywhere, not just after sign-in.
WHEN THE CHECK FAILS OUTRIGHT — THE FOURTH FORM
A composite, not one of your three. Page level carries the sentence once; the button carries the way out. Neither alone works: a per-row retry with a label becomes a wall on a ten-card list, and a page-level message alone leaves the buttons with no form.
The button becomes the OUTLINE of the same box — white fill, dark ink border, a refresh glyph, no label, at the same 128×46 / 112×44 / 124×44. Filled black means "we know" (+ Track / ✓ Tracked); filled-and-dimmed means "asking"; outlined means "we don't know". No word appears, so nothing is asserted, and it is a live control rather than a dead one.
One press fixes the page. Pressing any one of them refetches the whole list, so every unknown button resolves together. That is what makes ten of them tolerable — they are ten identical quiet boxes and one sentence, not ten errors.
We deliberately did NOT fall back to "+ Track". It is an assertion that can be wrong, and pressing it on an already-tracked bill would re-save rather than remove. Even with a refetch-on-press, the reader sees a claim about their own list that we have not earned.
Tone is neutral, not alarm. Grey notice, not red: nothing about the bill is wrong. The copy says so explicitly — "Everything about the bills themselves loaded normally — only your saved list is missing" — and never implies we will notify anyone. It is role="status", not alert.
Screen readers get the meaning, not the outline. Each button's aria-label is "Couldn't check whether you track this bill. Press to check again." — the visual treatment is doing the talking for sighted users, so the label has to say the same thing.
Confirmed: the unknown and failed forms are for a SIGNED-IN reader only. Signed out, the answer is known — they track nothing — so "+ Track" is correct and honest. Never build either form as a general loading or error state for the control.
SHOULD A DURATION THRESHOLD GOVERN IT?
Yes — but on the spinner, not on the state. Two separate things are being decided, and only one should be timed. The state is unconditional: the button must never claim "+ Track" before it knows, however brief the window, because a wrong assertion is worse than a blank one. So the dimmed fixed-size box shows from the first frame, always.
The spinner waits ~300ms. Motion that appears and vanishes inside a couple of hundred milliseconds registers as a flicker, and on a list of ten cards it is ten flickers. So hold the spinner back until the window exceeds roughly 300ms; under that, the reader sees only a briefly quiet button and nothing twitches. If your measurement comes back consistently under ~150ms, the spinner may never be worth showing at all — keep it in the build behind the threshold and let the number decide.

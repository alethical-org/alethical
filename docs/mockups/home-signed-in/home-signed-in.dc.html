New Council and Fund to Modernize Human Services IT

Establishes an advisory council of state, county, and Tribal representatives to help plan and prioritize upgrades to the computer systems counties and state agencies use to run human services programs, and a separate group of eight legislators to oversee that modernization work over the next several years. SF 5310In Committee
🔥 Hot issue
Bans Nonconsensual AI Nudification Images

Creates a new crime for making or distributing digitally altered or AI-generated images that realistically show a real, identifiable person nude, exposing intimate parts, or engaging in a sexual act, when that person did not consent to it and the original image did not show that. Penalties range up to five years in prison and a $10,000 fine, rising to ten years and $20,000 if aggravating factors are present, such as posting the image online, using it to harass someone, or maintaining a website built for creating or spreading such images. The bill lists exemptions for law enforcement, medical treatment, research, and reporting on matters of public interest, and it shields internet and telecom providers from liability for content posted by other people.

SF 5309In Committee
Creates Agricultural Land Ownership Oversight Office

Sets up a new Office of Business Regulation and Land Ownership within the Department of Agriculture. The office studies consolidation among agriculture-related businesses and evaluates trends in farmland purchases by individuals, companies, churches, and nonprofits, then advises the commissioner and reports findings to the legislature every two years. A director with expertise in antitrust and property law leads the office, supported by hired staff including lawyers and economists, and money is appropriated from the general fund to establish and run it starting in fiscal year 2027.

SF 5308In Committee
Rehiring Retired Police Officers Without Losing Pensions

Creates a special path for retired police officers to come back to work as police officers while keeping their full pension payments. To qualify, the officer must have at least five years of service in the plan, be receiving or have applied for a pension, be at least 55 years old, and wait at least a day after retiring before returning. While reemployed, the officer keeps making contributions but does not build up any additional pension credit, and the annuity amount stays fixed. The officer can also keep their state health insurance coverage, though they pay for it themselves unless a labor agreement says otherwise. Cities face limits on how many retired officers they can rehire this way, with the limits scaled to the size of the department.

# Grounded-answer invariants

Standing rules for any surface that generates, displays, or advertises answers. These outlive any single build — current implementation spec: `docs/product-onboarding/grounded-ask-spec.md`.

1. **Cite or refuse.** No generated answer ships without at least one citation resolving to an official source URL (bill → `Bill.official_url`, roll call → `VoteEvent.official_url`, legislator → `Legislator.profile_url`). Weak retrieval refuses rather than stretches. An honest "no matches" or "out of scope" is a first-class response, not an error state. For the generated **answer paths**, the standing enforcement of this contract is the acceptance suite `alethical/tests/test_ask_scenarios.py` (cite-or-refuse across `/ask` answer bodies, the grounded-neutrality authorship citations of rule 3, and the no-ungrounded-leak interim contract of rule 4), alongside the classification and `topic_bills` coverage in `alethical/tests/test_api_contract.py`.

2. **Never advertise what you can't answer.** Placeholders, sample question chips, suggested follow-ups, empty states, and marketing copy may only name intents the router can currently answer. System-suggested questions (chips) must be constructed so they cannot lead to a refusal.

3. **Grounded neutrality.** Describe records, never inferred positions: "authored / co-authored N bills," "voted yes on HF xxxx" — never "supports X" as an opinion claim. Use Minnesota Legislature terminology: author / co-author (not "sponsor"/"co-sponsor") in user-facing copy. This governs *displayed strings only* — code identifiers and the data model keep their names, including the genuine `SponsorshipRole.sponsor` enum value, which is a distinct role from chief/co-author (its semantics are tracked in `docs/product-onboarding/grounded-ask-spec.md` §4.2, Sponsorship display). Framing sentences that carry this rule are fixed UI copy owned by the layout, never LLM output.

4. **Records surfaces vs. generated answers.** Data surfaces (bill pages, Votes tab) render facts from the DB and may show anything the record contains. Generated answers carry the citation contract. Never leak record data (e.g., vote tallies) into a generated answer ahead of its cited answer path shipping.

5. **Anything linked to must be URL-addressable.** If a citation, CTA, or cross-page navigation targets a location (a tab, a highlighted passage, a roll-call row), that location must be reachable via URL (`?tab=votes`, `#anchor`) — component state cannot be linked from another page. Shareable URLs are a feature of every such surface, not an afterthought.

6. **Copy claims match shipped capability.** Product copy (hero subhead, deflection text, badges) asserts only what the shipped surfaces deliver — e.g., "how everyone voted" requires visible tallies + official-record links somewhere in the product. If a capability slips, trim the claim in the same release; never mock or ship copy around a gap.

7. **Answerable means ingested and fresh — keep the corpus current.** Every answer path is only as current as the ingested corpus: Grounded Ask can answer solely from what ingestion has actually pulled in, **at the state it was pulled in**. Any surface implying currency — the hero's "Minnesota law … where it stands," a "new law" framing, current-session chips — presumes the relevant session is in the corpus *and its records reflect reality*, so the **active / most-recent legislative session must be ingested and refreshed** before that copy ships (this is the currency face of rules 2 and 6). Staleness is the sneakier failure: ingestion is human-triggered and skips already-ingested bills by default, so a bill can sit in the corpus at a months-old status — production held HF 4138 at a pre-passage status two months after it was signed (found Jul 2026) — and a status-stale answer misframes enacted law as a pending proposal, violating the status-aware framing this product promises. Canonical current-law questions are **standing acceptance tests, not one-off examples**: "What's in the new social media law for kids?" must resolve to the enacted 2026 bill (HF 4138) with a cited answer whose framing says *law*, not *bill*. The corpus side of this shipped ([#155](https://github.com/alethical-org/alethical/issues/155), closed Jul 24 2026 — full 94th-biennium re-fetch, HF 4138 now reads as enacted); the end-to-end Ask check rides on [#79](https://github.com/alethical-org/alethical/issues/79). Closed does not mean permanently fixed — ingestion is human-triggered and `only_missing` by default, so this test is re-run, not retired. A persistent refusal on genuinely-enacted current law — or a confidently *stale* answer about it — is a **gap to close, not an acceptable steady state**: the refusal is still the correct behavior while a coverage gap exists (rule 1), but the gap is a bug against this rule, and it is the maintainer's job to notice a canonical use case has gone unanswerable or stale and fix the corpus, unprompted.

8. **Follow-up chat is a standing capability — answer-first, never router-opened.** Signed-in users can continue a single-bill answer conversationally: the bill page's chat entry keeps working, and the answer page's follow-up composer hands off into the existing bill-scoped chat seeded with the Ask question + generated answer (`docs/product-onboarding/grounded-ask-spec.md` §9.2, Answer page shared elements — decided Jul 2026). The hero/router never opens a chat session directly: every Ask produces a one-shot answer page first, and chat is a continuation surface, not a routing destination (there is deliberately no `chat` intent in the router). Changes to any Ask surface must not regress the bill-scoped chat — the standing enforcement is the contract tests `test_signed_in_chat_session_and_message_flow` and `test_bill_scoped_chat_missing_chunks_returns_grounded_fallback` (`alethical/tests/test_api_contract.py`).

9. **Bill summaries read as plain language — no bill-number prefix, no statute citations.** Wherever a bill's AI summary is *displayed* (cards, teasers, profile bill lists, search results), it must read as a plain-language sentence about what the bill *does* — never lead with the bill number ("The bill HF 577 …" / "HF 1116 …"; the identifier already shows in the amber code badge), and never carry raw statute citations ("Minnesota Statutes chapter 626", "section 297A.67, subdivision 40"), which read as legalese in a summary. Preferred: "Appropriates $6,000,000 from the bond proceeds fund to develop new facilities." The durable fix is at ingestion — the enrichment prompt generates summaries this way, and a populated `plain_language_summary` wins over the formal `summary`. **This regeneration landed in July 2026 (#520): the full corpus (10,471 bills) was re-enriched on `claude:claude-sonnet-5`, so stored summaries now read as plain language at source — 0% carry a bill-code prefix, and only ~0.08% of summaries (~0.9% of key points) retain an unavoidable statutory cross-reference, almost all recodification/repeal bills whose substance *is* that reference.** The shared display cleaners (`plainBillSummary` / `plainKeyPoints` in `apps/frontend/src/lib/billDetail.ts`) are **retained as defense-in-depth**, and since #710 are applied at **every** surface that displays a summary or key points — not a subset, so no surface can print a code preamble that the others strip.

**A display cleaner may only remove a citation where removing it cannot break the sentence — a leading amendatory clause, or a parenthetical aside (#710).** Breaking a sentence's grammar is itself a form of re-authoring, which this rule forbids. Stripping citations *anywhere* in the sentence was correct for the pre-#520 phrasing the cleaner was written against ("Amends Minnesota Statutes 2024, section 120B.123, to require …") and is wrong for the plain-language text the corpus now stores, where a citation left in the prose is load-bearing. Replayed across all 10,471 production summaries, the unrestricted rule damaged 9 of the 10 it touched — "formed under chapter 116A to the definition" → "formed under to the definition"; "like Section 8 vouchers" → "like vouchers"; "a federal change to section 179 business expensing" → "…to business expensing"; "Renames … throughout Minnesota Statutes." → "Renames … throughout." **"Section 8" and "section 179" are the names of a housing program and a federal tax provision, not Minnesota Statutes citations at all.** So the residual ~0.08% is *left as written*, which the paragraph above already anticipates: those are "almost all recodification/repeal bills whose substance *is* that reference." Position-limited stripping now changes 1 summary and 36 key-point lists corpus-wide, breaking none.

**Two standing tests for these cleaners, and they cover different things.** Since [#751](https://github.com/alethical-org/alethical/issues/751) the frontend has a test runner (Vitest), and `apps/frontend/src/lib/__tests__/billDetail.test.ts` pins every one of the nine production summaries the unrestricted citation strip damaged, as must-not-change cases that CI runs on every PR. That catches a re-broadened rule immediately, but only for the nine. **A corpus-wide change is still verified by the corpus replay** — transpile `lib/billDetail.ts`, run every production summary through old and new, and assert 0 newly-broken strings and 0 emptied summaries (method: memory `replay-corpus-through-transpiled-lib`). The committed tests do not replace it: they cannot see a summary nobody has thought of yet.

This governs *display only* — the stored record and the text the `/ask` answer path reasons over are untouched (rule 4).

10. **Plain language is the product, so it governs what you ADD to a surface, not just how summaries are written.** Rule 9 constrains generated text. This constrains proposals: before adding anything to a bill surface, check it against the plain-language promise, not only against accuracy. Two changes were proposed and rejected in one session on Jul 29 2026 ([#731](https://github.com/alethical-org/alethical/issues/731), closed not-planned), and **both were factually accurate and still wrong**, because accuracy was never the constraint they violated:

    - **An "AI summary" badge on every bill card.** Rejected: nothing is confusable in the first place (the bill page names its source, official text sits in its own **Bill Text** section, and generated answers must cite or refuse per rule 1), so the badge bought no separation and spent visual weight on every row of a long list. The scope doc's "clear distinction between official data and AI-generated analysis" (`docs/product-onboarding/product-scope.md` § Frontend Expectations) is met **structurally** — named sources, a separate official-text section, the citation contract — **not by per-element provenance chrome.**
    - **Displaying each bill's official statutory title** under the plain-language headline. Rejected harder: HF 2904's real title is a 400-character run-on of statutory cross-references, so surfacing it puts back precisely the legalese this product exists to remove. The official wording stays reachable (the **Bill Text** section, and the source link to revisor.mn.gov) without being pushed at the reader. This is why `BillHeader.tsx` passes the full title only as a hover tooltip and screen-reader label — that is deliberate, not an oversight to "fix."

    The test to apply: *does this help someone understand what the bill does, or does it only prove we know the legal wording?* The second kind belongs behind the source link, never on the page. Anything that makes the plain-language sentence harder to reach — a badge before it, boilerplate above it, a citation inside it — loses, even when it is true.

# Grounded-answer invariants

Standing rules for any surface that generates, displays, or advertises answers. These outlive any single build — current implementation spec: `docs/product-onboarding/grounded-ask-spec.md`.

**Why these exist.** They are `docs/philosophy.md` made checkable — principle 1 (truth before voice), 2 (uncover, don't translate), 4 (say only what we can do) and 5 (meet people in their own words), turned into things a test or a reviewer can fail a PR on. Read the philosophy when a rule below feels arbitrary, or when you hit a case it doesn't name; read the rule when you need the line that actually decides.

1. **Cite or refuse.** No generated answer ships without at least one citation resolving to an official source URL (bill → `Bill.official_url`, roll call → `VoteEvent.official_url`, legislator → `Legislator.profile_url`). Weak retrieval refuses rather than stretches. An honest "no matches" or "out of scope" is a first-class response, not an error state. For the generated **answer paths**, the standing enforcement of this contract is the acceptance suite `alethical/tests/test_ask_scenarios.py` (cite-or-refuse across `/ask` answer bodies, the grounded-neutrality authorship citations of rule 3, and the no-ungrounded-leak interim contract of rule 4), alongside the classification and `topic_bills` coverage in `alethical/tests/test_api_contract.py`.

2. **Never advertise what you can't answer.** Placeholders, sample question chips, suggested follow-ups, empty states, and marketing copy may only name intents the router can currently answer. System-suggested questions (chips) must be constructed so they cannot lead to a refusal.

3. **Grounded neutrality.** Describe records and source-backed relationships, never inferred positions or causes: "authored / co-authored N bills," "voted yes on HF xxxx" — never "supports X" as an opinion claim. Official campaign-finance filings, lobbying disclosures, and the relationships those records explicitly name are records too; Alethical may show and connect them. **Influence tracking means tracking documented money, relationships, and activity, not deciding that one caused another.** Never claim that a contribution or disclosed relationship caused a vote, reveals a hidden motive, proves influence, or establishes corruption unless a source directly supports that specific claim. Use Minnesota Legislature terminology: author / co-author (not "sponsor"/"co-sponsor") in user-facing copy. This governs *displayed strings only* — code identifiers and the data model keep their names, including the genuine `SponsorshipRole.sponsor` enum value, which is a distinct role from chief/co-author (its semantics are tracked in `docs/product-onboarding/grounded-ask-spec.md` §4.2, Sponsorship display). Framing sentences that carry this rule are fixed UI copy owned by the layout, never LLM output.

4. **Records surfaces vs. generated answers.** Data surfaces (bill pages, Votes tab) render facts from the DB and may show anything the record contains. Generated answers carry the citation contract. Never leak record data (e.g., vote tallies) into a generated answer ahead of its cited answer path shipping.

5. **Anything linked to must be URL-addressable.** If a citation, CTA, or cross-page navigation targets a location (a tab, a highlighted passage, a roll-call row), that location must be reachable via URL (`?tab=votes`, `#anchor`) — component state cannot be linked from another page. Shareable URLs are a feature of every such surface, not an afterthought.

6. **Copy claims match shipped capability.** Product copy (hero subhead, deflection text, badges) asserts only what the shipped surfaces deliver — e.g., "how everyone voted" requires visible tallies + official-record links somewhere in the product. If a capability slips, trim the claim in the same release; never mock or ship copy around a gap.

7. **Answerable means ingested and fresh — keep the corpus current.** Every answer path is only as current as the ingested corpus: Grounded Ask can answer solely from what ingestion has actually pulled in, **at the state it was pulled in**. Any surface implying currency — the hero's "Minnesota law … where it stands," a "new law" framing, current-session chips — presumes the relevant session is in the corpus *and its records reflect reality*, so the **active / most-recent legislative session must be ingested and refreshed** before that copy ships (this is the currency face of rules 2 and 6). Staleness is the sneakier failure: ingestion is human-triggered and skips already-ingested bills by default, so a bill can sit in the corpus at a months-old status — production held HF 4138 at a pre-passage status two months after it was signed (found Jul 2026) — and a status-stale answer misframes enacted law as a pending proposal, violating the status-aware framing this product promises. Canonical current-law questions are **standing acceptance tests, not one-off examples**: "What's in the new social media law for kids?" must resolve to the enacted 2026 bill (HF 4138) with a cited answer whose framing says *law*, not *bill*. The corpus side of this shipped ([#155](https://github.com/alethical-org/alethical/issues/155), closed Jul 24 2026 — full 94th-biennium re-fetch, HF 4138 now reads as enacted); the end-to-end Ask check rides on [#79](https://github.com/alethical-org/alethical/issues/79). Closed does not mean permanently fixed — ingestion is human-triggered and `only_missing` by default, so this test is re-run, not retired. A persistent refusal on genuinely-enacted current law — or a confidently *stale* answer about it — is a **gap to close, not an acceptable steady state**: the refusal is still the correct behavior while a coverage gap exists (rule 1), but the gap is a bug against this rule, and it is the maintainer's job to notice a canonical use case has gone unanswerable or stale and fix the corpus, unprompted.

8. **Follow-up chat is a standing capability — answer-first, never router-opened.** The *intent*, unchanged: signed-in users can continue a single-bill answer conversationally, and the answer page's follow-up composer hands off into the existing bill-scoped chat seeded with the Ask question + generated answer (`docs/product-onboarding/grounded-ask-spec.md` §9.2, Answer page shared elements — decided Jul 2026). The hero/router never opens a chat session directly: every Ask produces a one-shot answer page first, and chat is a continuation surface, not a routing destination (there is deliberately no `chat` intent in the router). Changes to any Ask surface must not regress the bill-scoped chat — the standing enforcement is the contract tests `test_signed_in_chat_session_and_message_flow` and `test_bill_scoped_chat_missing_chunks_returns_grounded_fallback` (`alethical/tests/test_api_contract.py`).

    **No reader can reach it today. This rule claimed "the bill page's chat entry keeps working" through two changes that removed the entry ([#1032](https://github.com/alethical-org/alethical/issues/1032)), and because this file loads into every session, that one clause is the likeliest reason nobody noticed.** What is actually true, verified on `main` Aug 6 2026:

    - **The server is fine.** `POST /me/chat-sessions`, `GET /me/chat-sessions`, `GET /me/chat-sessions/{id}` all live in `alethical/api/routers/me.py`, and both contract tests above pass. Nothing here is about broken backend code.
    - **The client screen is fine too, and can still create a bill-scoped session.** `ChatSessionScreen.tsx` calls `useCreateChatSession` when it receives `subjectType: 'bill'` + `subjectId` + `title` (from route params or its `sessionStorage` hand-off), and it is still registered in `RootNavigator.tsx`. So this is a **missing door on a working room**, not a missing room — do not "rebuild chat".
    - **What is missing is any way in.** Every chat URL is redirected to Home by `apps/frontend/src/navigation/webRoutes.ts` — `/chat`, `/chat/new`, and `/chat/sessions/{id}` all return `{ kind: 'tab', screen: 'Home' }`. The Chat tab is also excluded from `VISIBLE_TABS`. Separately, the in-app entry point died with `BillDetailScreen.tsx`, deleted by [#1071](https://github.com/alethical-org/alethical/pull/1071).

    **Before building the composer, read `docs/product-onboarding/grounded-ask-spec.md` §9.5 (chip-reached answer page). It constrains where a composer may go, and it wins for the `bill_text` state by its own opening sentence.** Its Scope paragraph specifies "no question field, no Ask button, no composer" on that page, and makes the page **signed-out only** with a Bill Detail chip as the only way in.

    **Read §9.5's deferred-items table before concluding the capability is parked — it is not, and an earlier version of this rule got this wrong.** That table's columns are `Upgrade | Issue | Today | Why deferred`, and its `Today` column describes what ships now. For [#137](https://github.com/alethical-org/alethical/issues/137) it reads "Submitting navigates to the existing chat screen, seeded with the question + answer (§9.2)", and for [#84](https://github.com/alethical-org/alethical/issues/84) "only `bill_text` answers thread (into existing bill-scoped chat, §9.2)". So what is deferred is **rendering the thread in place** (#137) and **topic threading** (#84). **The hand-off into bill-scoped chat is current design, not a deferred upgrade** — reading Scope's one "auth-gated follow-up model stays deferred" sentence as parking the whole capability contradicts the table two hundred lines below it.

    **The real gap is narrower and it is a genuine hole, not deliberate latitude: no section says where a *signed-in* reader continues a bill conversation.** §9.5 governs a signed-out page; nothing governs the signed-in equivalent. Treat that as unspecified and get it framed by Design ([#1032](https://github.com/alethical-org/alethical/issues/1032)) rather than choosing placement, label or wording in code — §9.2 pins the Track button to hex values and pixel dimensions while saying nothing about the composer's appearance, so the silence is an omission rather than freedom.

9. **Bill summaries read as plain language — no bill-number prefix, no statute citations.** Wherever a bill's AI summary is *displayed* (cards, teasers, profile bill lists, search results), it must read as a plain-language sentence about what the bill *does* — never lead with the bill number ("The bill HF 577 …" / "HF 1116 …"; the identifier already shows in the amber code badge), and never carry raw statute citations ("Minnesota Statutes chapter 626", "section 297A.67, subdivision 40"), which read as legalese in a summary. Preferred: "Appropriates $6,000,000 from the bond proceeds fund to develop new facilities." The durable fix is at ingestion — the enrichment prompt generates summaries this way, and a populated `plain_language_summary` wins over the formal `summary`.

**That last clause was aspirational until #766 — nothing read the field for months.** The enrichment wrote both summaries for all 10,471 bills; `ai_analysis_payload_for_enrichment` read `summary` only, so every surface showed the formal one. `display_summary` (`alethical/api/serializers.py`) now implements the preference with a fallback, so a bill with only a formal summary still shows it. Measured before switching, across all 10,471 bills: the two fields differ on **10,115**, and the plain-language one carries **8** statute citations against 81, **691** raw 7-digit dollar figures against 1,445, is ~60 characters shorter on average, and is never empty. A reproducible 20-bill read scored it plainer on 13, worse on 5, level on 2. **One deliberate exception:** the Ask router's bill-disambiguation step (`_resolve_bill_by_content`, `alethical/api/routers/ask.py`) keeps reading the formal `summary`, because that text is an LLM disambiguation aid whose specifics separate near-duplicate bills, and changing what it reasons over moves an eval-gated path (rule 1's acceptance suite; retrieval quality on [#400](https://github.com/alethical-org/alethical/issues/400)). Both halves are pinned by `test_display_summary_prefers_plain_language_and_ask_keeps_the_formal_one`. **This regeneration landed in July 2026 (#520): the full corpus (10,471 bills) was re-enriched on `claude:claude-sonnet-5`, so stored summaries now read as plain language at source — 0% carry a bill-code prefix, and only ~0.08% of summaries (~0.9% of key points) retain an unavoidable statutory cross-reference, almost all recodification/repeal bills whose substance *is* that reference.** The shared display cleaners (`plainBillSummary` / `plainKeyPoints` in `apps/frontend/src/lib/billDetail.ts`) are **retained as defense-in-depth**, and since #710 are applied at **every** surface that displays a summary or key points — not a subset, so no surface can print a code preamble that the others strip.

**A display cleaner may only remove a citation where removing it cannot break the sentence — a leading amendatory clause, or a parenthetical aside (#710).** Breaking a sentence's grammar is itself a form of re-authoring, which this rule forbids. Stripping citations *anywhere* in the sentence was correct for the pre-#520 phrasing the cleaner was written against ("Amends Minnesota Statutes 2024, section 120B.123, to require …") and is wrong for the plain-language text the corpus now stores, where a citation left in the prose is load-bearing. Replayed across all 10,471 production summaries, the unrestricted rule damaged 9 of the 10 it touched — "formed under chapter 116A to the definition" → "formed under to the definition"; "like Section 8 vouchers" → "like vouchers"; "a federal change to section 179 business expensing" → "…to business expensing"; "Renames … throughout Minnesota Statutes." → "Renames … throughout." **"Section 8" and "section 179" are the names of a housing program and a federal tax provision, not Minnesota Statutes citations at all.** So the residual ~0.08% is *left as written*, which the paragraph above already anticipates: those are "almost all recodification/repeal bills whose substance *is* that reference." Position-limited stripping now changes 1 summary and 36 key-point lists corpus-wide, breaking none.

**Two standing tests for these cleaners, and they cover different things.** Since [#751](https://github.com/alethical-org/alethical/issues/751) the frontend has a test runner (Vitest), and `apps/frontend/src/lib/__tests__/billDetail.test.ts` pins every one of the nine production summaries the unrestricted citation strip damaged, as must-not-change cases that CI runs on every PR. That catches a re-broadened rule immediately, but only for the nine. **A corpus-wide change is still verified by the corpus replay** — transpile `lib/billDetail.ts`, run every production summary through old and new, and assert 0 newly-broken strings and 0 emptied summaries (method: memory `replay-corpus-through-transpiled-lib`). The committed tests do not replace it: they cannot see a summary nobody has thought of yet.

This governs *display only* — the stored record and the text the `/ask` answer path reasons over are untouched (rule 4).

10. **Plain language is the product, so it governs what you ADD to a surface, not just how summaries are written.** Rule 9 constrains generated text. This constrains proposals: before adding anything to a bill surface, check it against the plain-language promise, not only against accuracy. Two changes were proposed and rejected in one session on Jul 29 2026 ([#731](https://github.com/alethical-org/alethical/issues/731), closed not-planned), and **both were factually accurate and still wrong**, because accuracy was never the constraint they violated:

    - **An "AI summary" badge on every bill card.** Rejected: nothing is confusable in the first place (the bill page names its source, official text sits in its own **Bill Text** section, and generated answers must cite or refuse per rule 1), so the badge bought no separation and spent visual weight on every row of a long list. The scope doc's "clear distinction between official data and AI-generated analysis" (`docs/product-onboarding/product-scope.md` § Frontend Expectations) is met **structurally** — named sources, a separate official-text section, the citation contract — **not by per-element provenance chrome.**
    - **Displaying each bill's official statutory title** under the plain-language headline. Rejected harder: HF 2904's real title is a 400-character run-on of statutory cross-references, so surfacing it puts back precisely the legalese this product exists to remove. The official wording stays reachable (the **Bill Text** section, and the source link to revisor.mn.gov) without being pushed at the reader. This is why `BillHeader.tsx` passes the full title as a hover tooltip — that is deliberate, not an oversight to "fix."

    **The screen-reader half of that sentence was wrong, and it inverted the rule it was defending.** This clause used to bless passing the statutory title as the *screen-reader label* too. `accessibilityLabel` **replaces** an element's visible text for assistive technology rather than adding to it, so a bill page showed 43 plain-language characters on screen and announced **900 characters of statutory cross-references** to anyone using a screen reader — measured on production 11 Aug 2026 from Chrome's accessibility tree, which is what a screen reader is handed. The one group who cannot see the plain-language headline was the only group receiving the legalese, from a rule whose entire purpose is keeping legalese out of the reader's way. Compounding it, [#1355](https://github.com/alethical-org/alethical/issues/1355) made that title the page's only top-level heading, so heading navigation — how a screen-reader user skims — landed there first.

    **Ruled ([#1362](https://github.com/alethical-org/alethical/issues/1362)): the heading announces the plain-language title, the same words everyone else reads.** The statutory wording stays reachable exactly where this rule already puts it, in the **Bill Text** section and behind the source link. Nothing is hidden; it simply stops being the first thing a screen-reader user hears. The general lesson, which is why this correction is written out rather than quietly edited: an accessibility label is not a free place to put extra text. It is a *substitution*, so anything you put there is what that reader gets **instead of** what everyone else gets. Check the computed accessible name rather than the attribute you wrote (memory `read-the-computed-accessibility-name`).

    The test to apply: *does this help someone understand what the bill does, or does it only prove we know the legal wording?* The second kind belongs behind the source link, never on the page. Anything that makes the plain-language sentence harder to reach — a badge before it, boilerplate above it, a citation inside it — loses, even when it is true.

11. **An answer may only speak for as much of a bill as it read, and may never vouch for its own list.** Two claims, and the difference between them is the whole rule:

    - **Absence.** "The bill does not specify any counties" is a claim about the whole bill. It is honest only when the whole bill was read. Read four passages of a hundred and the same sentence turns absence-from-a-sample into absence-from-the-bill.
    - **Completeness.** "This list includes all the cities named" is a claim about the model's own enumeration. **Nothing can verify that, so it is never honest** — not even after reading every word, because reading everything is not reporting everything.

    Both are **enforced in the answer path, not merely requested in the prompt** (`alethical/api/routers/me.py`): a prompt is a request, and the shipped bug was a model doing exactly as instructed. An absence claim is re-scoped to the text actually searched when the read was partial, and left alone when it was complete, where it is true and useful; a completeness claim is removed whatever the coverage. Every phrasing either guard handles is pinned by a case in `alethical/tests/test_ask_scenarios.py`, alongside must-not-change cases — these guards edit displayed model output, so rule 9's limit binds them too: only an edit that cannot break the sentence, which is why one swaps a singular subject for a singular subject and the other deletes a whole sentence rather than editing inside one.

    What replaces the false assurance is a **layout-owned note above the answer**, driven by a served fact and worded by the page (`docs/product-onboarding/grounded-ask-spec.md` §9.5 decision 11). Above, not below: the reader most at risk of being misled is the one who reads least, and a caveat after the list is seen only by readers who did not need it. This is the division of labour rule 3 already sets — the model describes records, the layout owns the framing — and here the layout is the only half that *knows* the scope.

    **Reading more of the bill is not a substitute for either.** Measured two ways: the #865 eval found the overclaim rate flat at 80% / 89% / 80% across 4, 8 and 16 passages, and given four times the text one model produced a longer list with a *stronger* completeness claim; separately, given all 102 passages of HF 719 the writer listed 26-35 of its 98 cities. Wider windows buy completeness where it is affordable. They do not buy honesty, and a change that widens coverage must check it has not removed a caveat as a side effect of making `used == total`.

    **And no count of the things asked about, however derived.** §9.5 decision 5 used to permit a layout-computed label ("Nineteen cities are named"), reasoning that a count of what is on screen cannot be wrong about what is on screen. True, and beside the point: a reader takes "19" to mean nineteen exist. HF 719 names 98 cities; the answer listed 19. **A count computed honestly from a partial list is still a fabricated fact**, and it lands in the largest type on the page.

    Origin ([#868](https://github.com/alethical-org/alethical/issues/868), Jul 31 2026): production answered "which cities and counties get named infrastructure grants?" for HF 719 with nineteen cities and "the bill does not specify any counties." The bill names **98 cities and 20 counties** (counted in `alethical/eval/ground_truth.py`). Every trust signal fired correctly on it — the citations were real, resolvable, and pointed at the four passages the answer had genuinely read — which is what made it the worst failure this product can produce: not an answer that looks uncertain and is wrong, but one that looks verified and is wrong.

12. **Campaign-finance figures: two numbers, and never a chain.** Rule 3 already forbids saying money *caused* anything. This is the display half, and it exists because both failures below are ways of being wrong while every individual number on screen is correct.

    - **The reported total and the sum of listed payments are different figures, and both are shown.** Minnesota names a donor only once their giving passes $200 in aggregate **within a calendar year** (Minnesota Statutes 10A.20 subd. 3(c); $500 for ballot questions); everything below that reaches us as one unnamed lump on the filing. Read that threshold precisely, because the plausible misreading has already been drafted for a real screen: it is a test on the donor's yearly total, never on the size of a gift, so **no surface may say that gifts of $200 or less are never named** when 327,759 of the 583,152 published rows are individually under $200. The sentence a page may print is "donors who gave $200 or less in total for the year are never named." So the payments we can list never add up to the official total, and the gap is legitimate money rather than an error or a hole in our data. A surface that prints only the sum of visible rows understates the truth and looks authoritative doing it. Show both and say what the difference is.
    - **A missing value reads "Not reported". A verified zero reads "0".** A candidate who genuinely raised nothing is not a candidate whose filing we do not hold, and collapsing the two invents a fact about a named person.
    - **Separate transfers, never a chain.** That a party gave a caucus $100,000 and the caucus later gave a candidate $5,000 are two documented facts. That the same dollars travelled between them is not a fact, and no filing establishes it — money is fungible, and once it lands in an account no record says which dollar went out. Show each transfer with its own amount, date and source. A network view may show the shape; it may not imply continuation, and no label may assert the meaning (no "pay-to-play", no "funding signature").

    Dates are three rules, not one: each payment shows its own payment date and source; each total states the period it covers and its source; each page shows one clearly labelled freshness date ([#861](https://github.com/alethical-org/alethical/issues/861)).
